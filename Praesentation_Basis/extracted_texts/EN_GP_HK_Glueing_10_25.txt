Unpack and pre-install RENNER hammershanks
▪ Unpack the RENNER hammershanks
▪ Pre-install in fields according to the distribution of the 
instrument
▪ Let one or two pieces off between the fields
▪ Start with treble as packed by RENNER
▪ If used action rail, turn the screw at first in left direction to snap 
in place to the old thread pitch
▪ When found tighten the screw lightly with fingers
▪ Fix all hammershanks like this
▪ Our recommendation: use new RENNER screws for RENNER 
actions: for washers as disc springs, clean cross recess and 
combination slot and cross (maximum benefits)
Required tools: 
➢ GP action rack for RENNER agencys and repair workshops with 
different action rails for hammer gluing
Required material: 
➢ Screw 4,5x22mm for flanges (grand shank use), with spring 
washer and combined screw slot Item number: 43-0008745
➢ RENNER hammerheads and hammershanks

Unpack Renner hammerhead set, count, sort and number 
▪ Open the set pac
▪ Put the hammerheads on a working board
▪ Sort out the surplus hammerheads
▪ Every RENNER hammerhead 
is signed with its number in 
the set and RENNER 
branding ex works (except
      original Steinway spareparts)
▪ If the hammerhead moulding 
is tapered (thinner then 
cutting width), sometimes 
the number is sanded away
▪ If you put out hammerheads 
when sorting before gluing 
numbers get lost
➢ So notice: 
it is absolutly necessary to sign 
new numbers on the 
hammerheads

--- PAGE 19 ---
Control gluing of hammeheads
▪ If hot glue has been used, treat with a brush and hot water to
      ensure a perfect glue joint
▪ If cold glue has been used, you can do the same with water 
      diluted cold glue (we call that „glue water“)

--- PAGE 20 ---
Plastering the glue rings
▪ Cut with a knife
▪ After approx one hour of drying 
      time, the glue has the optimum 
      strength (like vine gum) to 
      plaster over any glue residue 
      that has run     
▪ Put the rest away by hand

--- PAGE 24 ---
Grind down | Sanding | Forming hammer tails
▪ Step 1: grind down 
      overhangs
▪ Step 2: sanding an 
      even surface
▪ Step 3: form out the
      hammer tail 
▪ Not to round 
      because can be 
      pop up/ no good catch
▪ Not to flat because
      hooking possible ▪ Fully formed hammer tails

--- PAGE 25 ---
Re-cleaning by hand
▪ Refinishing with a sanding block, 
      grain size 40, to create a better grip ▪ Rework the back site edge if suspension springs are on the whippens
▪ Do this also, if you´re not shure, if springs could be on the wippens

--- PAGE 26 ---
Cosmetics
▪ Plaster with a fine sandpaper 
      file, break the edges and 
      corners
▪ Now work is done, and you 
      have a last chance to correct
      passage of hammers with 
      burner lamp
▪ Unscrew the hammershanks 
      and pack for sending them to 
      customer, or install them on
      the action and begin regulation 
      works
