History of Weickert felt
• Foundet in 1783 by J.D. Weickert in 
the town Leipzig in Saxony
• In 1847 the first hammerhead felt 
was produced in new factory 
Wurzen, that could be used like 
today
• Already a suplplier to many well 
known manufactures

Raw wool and wool fibres for RENNER hammerhead
• Raw wool delivered in bales
• The wool must not be pressed too hard to avoid 
damaging the wool fibres
• This increases transport costs but also the 
quality
• If the wool fibres are strongly curled, this is 
an advantage for fulling during the felt 
production

--- PAGE 9 ---
The felt board for RENNER hammerhead
• Felt boards are compressed until the fibers can 
no longer be pushed together
• Corresponds to maximum elasticity
• Maximum natural walking hardness is 0.56 
SHORE hardness
• Post-processing is done by compressing the felt 
boards with hammers and felt stones
• Re-pressing to the desired shore hardness and 
specified dimensions to the final size
• Without sanding and without reinforced pressing.
• This results in the greatest possible elasticity in the 
felt board
• This is recognizable by a smooth surface when 
bending

Principle of hammer to shenk angle
Principle: 
• The hammer should hit at a right angle
to the plane of the strings.
• The hammer shank should be able to 
make a 3 mm overhang.
• The effect is that due to the inertia of the 
material, the hammer excites the energy 
in the direction of the bridge more.
• If the angle becomes smaller than 90°
when hit the string, the hammer gets 
wedged towards to the agrafe and the 
string can break and affecting the tone.
When does this happens mostly?
• After repairing old hammerheads, when to 
much felt is sanded from the hammerhead.
• Good argument to sell new hammers to 
your customer.

--- PAGE 32 ---
Calculation of boreing length
(1.) Distance from string plane to keybed
- (2.) Distance hammer shank axis (center pin) to keybed
= Difference between both values 
- (3.) 3 mm (for overhang shank when hits string)
= (4.) drilling length
