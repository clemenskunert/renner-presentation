## Chapter 2: The Art of Felt Manufacturing – FFW Wurzen (Slides 05–10 · approx. 12–15 min)

- `🎙️ Spoken Narrative:` *“Filzfabrik Wurzen is not an ordinary supplier — founded in 1783 in Saxony’s Mulde valley, it is the oldest industrial felt manufactory in the world. Right here in 1847, deer leather on piano hammers was replaced by German hammerhead felt, and right here the legendary ‘Weickert Spezial’ formula was born. The fact that Wurzen and our Meuselwitz plant lie just 65 kilometers apart gives us a priceless advantage: our master hammermakers and Wurzen’s master feltmakers calibrate every batch together face to face.”*
- **All 6 Historical Era Tabs at a Glance:**
  1. **1783 – Origin in Wurzen (`05.1`):** Johann Dankegott Weickert establishes Saxony’s wool and felt tradition — specializing early in highly elastic fine felts for piano building.
  2. **1847 – First German Piano Hammer Felt (`05.2`):** **Patzschke & Weickert** replace inconsistent leather coverings with homogeneous piano hammer felt (Golzern Mill 1850; aided decisively by the soft, low-mineral water of the Mulde river).
  3. **1850/1851/1867 – World Exhibition Gold (`05.3`):** Gold medals in Leipzig (1850), London (1851), and Paris (1867) alongside royal visits; leading makers in Vienna, Paris, and London convert to Weickert felt.
  4. **1861 – Industrial Fulling on the Mühlgraben (`05.4`):** In-house steam power, precision fulling mills, and gentle wool scouring in mineral-poor Mulde water establish reproducible batch consistency.
  5. **1906 – Global Market Leader for `200,000` Pianos/Year (`05.5`):** Prior to 1914, Director Patzschke and 350 specialists equip up to 200,000 instruments worldwide every year — serving as the benchmark for Steinway & Sons New York.
  6. **Today – Revival of “Weickert Spezial” & Renner Partnership (`05.6`):** In **2010**, the Brand family revives the authentic 1903 recipe exclusively for concert demands; **just `~65 km` distance** to Renner Meuselwitz enables direct master-to-master collaboration without long supply chains.
- **Right Card (4 Acoustic Secrets of Weickert Felt):** 100% pure virgin Merino wool · pure mechanical fulling with zero resins/glues · millisecond elastic rebound of helical keratin springs · `65 km` proximity Wurzen ↔ Meuselwitz.

### Slide 06 — Pure Merino Wool & the Secret of Fiber Blending
- `🎙️ Spoken Narrative:` *“Why does Weickert felt sound so alive and stay dimensionally stable across decades? Everything begins with how the raw wool is shipped and scoured. Our overseas Merino wool arrives in Wurzen deliberately in uncompressed bales so the microscopic keratin cuticle scales and strong natural crimp are never crushed. Equally vital is gentle scouring that preserves natural wool grease — lanolin — inside the hair. In Stage 2, the master feltmakers blend long, high-tensile fibers for outer crown tension with fine filler wool for resilient core support.”*
- **The 2 Process Stages on Screen:**
  - **Stage 01 – Raw Wool Selection:** Loosely packed, **uncompressed overseas Merino wool** (Argentina, South Africa, New Zealand/Australia) · pronounced natural **helical crimp** acting like millions of micro-springs · preserved **natural lanolin** keeping fibers supple for decades.
  - **Stage 02 – Fiber Formula in the FFW Blending Chamber:** Multi-stage gentle plucking and **pneumatic opening** · controlled **climate conditioning** preventing static buildup, fiber breakage, and dense knots · exact balance of **long tensile fibers** (crown) and **fine filler wool** (core cushion).

### Slide 07 — Carding: Parallel Fiber Alignment into Fine Fleece Webs
- `🎙️ Spoken Narrative:` *“After blending, the wool moves onto historic drum cards. Fine rollers gently comb the fibers parallel into a gossamer-thin fleece web and stack them in defined layers. For every piano technician and voicer in this room, right here lies a crucial secret: only because these fibers were combed cleanly parallel during carding does your voicing needle later glide smoothly between the fibers and part them gently, instead of severing tangled fibers!”*
- **Key Points to Emphasize:**
  - **Drum Cards (Carding):** Comb millions of Merino fibers without breakage into a defined primary fiber course.
  - **Cross-Layered Stacking:** Builds sheet thickness, tensile strength, and the tapered wedge profile while preventing delamination.
  - **Direct Link to Voicing (Chapter 5):** Parallel fiber alignment = predictable, uniform tonal response to every single needle stitch.

### Slide 08 — The 4 Pillars of Fulling: Steam, Pressure, Motion & Experience
- `🎙️ Spoken Narrative:` *“Now the loose stack of fleece webs enters the hammer fulling mill and becomes true piano hammer felt — driven solely by four physical and artisanal pillars: First, live steam opens the microscopic scales of the wool hair. Second, mechanical pressure compresses the stacked webs. Third, oscillating friction interlocks the barbed scales permanently like burrs. And fourth, the master fuller’s trained touch pinpoints the exact second to stop. We use only soft Muldental water and heat — zero synthetic resins, zero chemical binders that would damp the tone.”*
- **The 4 Bento Cards Exactly as Shown on Screen:**
  1. **1. Live Steam:** Opens microscopic cuticle scales and renders the keratin supple.
  2. **2. Mechanical Pressure:** Progressively compresses the cross-laid fleece webs.
  3. **3. Oscillating Motion:** Rhythmic friction micro-mechanically and permanently interlocks the scales.
  4. **4. Master Experience:** Determines the exact stopping point for maximum natural springback and overtone purity.

### Slide 09 — 56% Fulling Density (0.56 g/cm³): The Natural Limit of Mechanical Fulling
- `🎙️ Spoken Narrative:` *“How far can pure Merino wool be compacted purely mechanically? Nature sets the physical ceiling at exactly 56 percent fulling density — 0.56 grams per cubic centimeter. Stop too early, and the hammer lacks internal spring tension. Try to force compaction mechanically beyond 56 percent, and the hollow keratin fibers crush and fracture — what feltmakers call felt breakage, permanently destroying lively rebound.”*
- **Technical Takeaway:**
  - **`56%` (`0.56 g/cm³`) = Physical Optimum:** Maximum fiber-to-fiber support without structural crushing → shortest string contact times, blooming tone without treble harshness, and lasting density across tens of thousands of strikes.

### Slide 10 — Final Compaction & Tapered 36-Layer Anatomy
- `🖱️ Interaction:` Toggle the animated cross-section on the right between **`Unsanded • Shape-Stable`** (`10.1`) and **`Sanded • Deformation`** (`10.2`)!
- `🎙️ Spoken Narrative:` *“Slide 10 reveals one of the most decisive quality hallmarks of Wurzen Weickert felt. - **Comparison of Both Interactive States:**
  - **`10.1` Unsanded (Renner / Weickert):** `2 × 6` continuous outer webs fully enclose the stepped inner webs → closed bending radius around the **hammer moulding**, 100% preservation of outer tensile tautness.
  - **`10.2` Sanded Sheet:** Outer cover webs severed → fibers slip apart under bending tension, causing crown splay and loss of structural prestress.

### Slide 15 — Optimal Hammerhead Weight Relative to String Weight
- `🖱️ Interaction:` Step through the zone buttons **`① Bass (~15 g)`**, **`② Break (−1 g)`**, **`③ Treble (down to 5 g)`**; optionally click any key (`A₀–C₈`) and test **`Too Light` / `Equilibrium` / `Too Heavy`** + **`Test Strike`** to demonstrate contact time `τc` and string deflection.
- `🎙️ Spoken Narrative:` *“Why is low wood mass so critical in the treble, yet governed by completely different physics in the bass? Slide 15 plots both curves on the exact same chart: vibrating string mass and hammerhead weight. In the bass, a single copper-wound string weighs between 90 and over 200 grams — meaning our hammerhead at 15 down to 13 grams is always comparatively light relative to the string! That is why we hold hammerhead weight high as long as possible across the entire bass section to drive maximum fundamental deflection. Moving from Key 23 to Key 24, we cross the break onto plain steel trichords weighing only around 31 grams combined. Because we kept the bass deliberately high, we execute a calibrated one-gram step right here from 13 down to 12 grams! And across the top two octaves up to C8, all three steel wires together drop below 3 grams down to just 0.6 grams, with a half-period shorter than half a millisecond. If the hammer were too heavy here, it would linger on the string and choke its own tone — so we taper smoothly down to 5.0 grams using lightweight American walnut.”*
- **The 3 Register Scaling Laws:**
  1. **① Bass (`Keys 1–23`, copper-wound `90–210 g`):** Hold hammer mass (`~15.0 → 13.0 g`) high as long as possible for deep fundamental power.
  2. **② Break (`Key 23 → 24`, plain steel `3 strings ≈ 31 g`):** Calibrated **`−1.0 g` step** (`13.0 → 12.0 g`) for a seamless register transition.
  3. **③ Treble (`last 2 octaves to C₈`):** Choir mass drops from `2.7 g` (`C₆`) to `~0.6 g` (`C₈`, `~0.2 g`/wire, `½ T < 0.5 ms`) → smooth taper to **`5.0 g`** for instantaneous rebound without self-damping.

## Appendix: Master-Level Answers to Frequent Conference Questions (Q&A Reference)

### Workshop Q&A — Top 6 Technician Questions at Symposia & Masterclasses
- **Question 1: “Why does FFW Wurzen stop fulling at 56% (0.56 g/cm³) — aren’t harder felts available on the market?”**
  - **Answer:** `56%` is the natural physical ceiling of purely mechanical felting in pure Merino wool. Higher apparent hardness can only be forced by excessive mechanical pressing (which crushes hollow keratin fibers — **felt breakage**, killing elastic rebound) or by adding synthetic resins/chemical hardeners, which increase internal damping friction, narrow the overtone spectrum, and react unpredictably to humidity swings.
- **Question 2: “How does Weickert Special perform under extreme Asian humidity swings (humid summer/monsoon vs. dry heated winter)?”**
  - **Answer:** That is precisely what accredited **STFI Test Report P2024 0903** quantified: thanks to **`+38%` higher natural residual wool grease (`0.36%` lanolin per ISO 3074)**, the wool fiber absorbs dramatically less ambient moisture — thickness swelling during the 48-hour climate cycling test drops by **`65%`** (`4.81%` vs. `13.69%`). Voicing and regulation remain vastly more climate-stable.
- **Question 3: “Which exact models fit the Louis Renner Yamaha Kit, and does it still require voicing after installation?”**
  - **Answer:** The kit is custom-engineered for **Yamaha C1, C2, and C3** grand pianos. - **Question 4: “When do you recommend hornbeam versus American walnut for the hammer moulding?”**
  - **Answer:** **Hornbeam** (when gently and patiently seasoned) is our ideal, heavy, remarkably uniform all-round moulding wood delivering strong foundational projection. **American walnut** is our lightest, tightest-grained, longest-fibered premier tonewood — unbeatable wherever low rotational inertia, lightning-fast repetition, and a radiant, non-self-damping treble are paramount (including our Yamaha Kit).
- **Question 5: “Why must 12 o’clock crown stitches stay under 2 mm deep and strictly between the string grooves?”**
  - **Answer:** The outermost `2 × 6` unsanded fleece webs form the continuous tensile arch carrying the hammerhead’s rebound force. Piercing deeper than `2 mm` into the red `12:00` core severs that arch irreversibly (`τc` lengthens sharply and the tone turns hollow and dead in both *p* and *ff*). - **Question 6: “Why use a Braun Series 1 foil shaver on the crown instead of ordinary sandpaper?”**
  - **Answer:** Coarse sanding strips away precious outer tension-web material, altering both geometry and mass.
